Celeste RL

Teaching a reinforcement learning agent to play Celeste: the real Steam game, on unmodified physics, using only the game's own controls. I built the whole stack: a C# game mod that runs the game about 85× faster than the existing interface, a verified training environment, and pre-registered experiments scored on held-out states.

  • Without demonstrations, PPO cleared Room 1 zero times in 50,369 episodes. I measured why: the policy never left near-uniform play, and the reward gave almost no signal between failures.
  • With seven demonstrations found by a search, and no human play, cloning then PPO works. Training from varied starts beat the fixed start by 22.6 points on 200 held-out states (95% CI +6.7 to +43.5), in all six matched seeds.
  • The same design did not replicate in Room 2 (−5.8 points, CI −18.7 to +3.4). I published that too, and it led to a study of why fine-tuning runs collapse.

How it’s built

  • A C# Everest mod hooks CelesteTAS’s input playback and hands each frame to Python over a local socket, so the game runs as fast as the agent can act instead of at 60 Hz.
  • Resets restore a savestate in about 4 ms. A 30-minute soak ran 4.85 million steps with 808 of 808 replay checks identical.
  • Found and fixed a render-target leak in Everest’s dash trails that grew memory by 8 GB in 4 minutes.
  • 13 live fault checks, such as replacing the connection mid-reset or a client that stops reading, and a 30-check input-binding audit.

How it’s tested

  • Each experiment’s plan and analysis are committed before its data exists, and negative results are reported in full.
  • Scored on frozen held-out states, with confidence intervals that resample training seeds and whole routes, not single episodes.
  • Every run records its commit and the game’s file hashes, and refuses to start if either drifts.
  • When an early result turned out to rest on post-selected checkpoints, I withdrew it and replaced it with the corrected analysis.

#002

WCA Analysis

An analysis of the World Cube Association's results export, covering every official speedcubing competition and competitor on record. Ten questions across three notebooks, each answered and then tested against the obvious answer.

  • A trend model that fit the 3x3 world record with r² ≈ 0.95 still predicted the sub-3-second record 2.5 to 4+ years too early.
  • No home advantage. Comparing about 13,900 competitors against themselves, people perform closer to their best away from home (p = 1.3e-67).
  • Caught two data problems in the loaders, duplicate competitor rows and mixed-type event IDs, and pinned both with a pytest suite that runs in CI.

#003

DualSense Quick Menu

A PS5-style control centre for Windows. Press the PS button on a DualSense mid-game and an overlay opens for Spotify, volume, switching apps and sleep or shutdown, all without putting the controller down.

  • Browse Liked Songs and playlists, control playback, and see a Now Playing card with real album art.
  • A controller-driven Alt-Tab, built fresh from the live window list each time it opens.
  • In closed alpha. Every build runs the tests, packages with PyInstaller, then self-tests the finished exe before it's zipped.
